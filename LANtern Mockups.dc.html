2Redesign — the path becomes the subject

The move

Peer card → peer row

One peer per full-width row, 72px tall. The eight facts are sorted into three columns — who, how they connect, how well — so nothing is truncated and the five actions get real 36px targets instead of a crowded footer.

The badge becomes a drawing

Instead of the word "Relayed", each row draws its actual path: you → hop → them. A direct peer has one line. A relayed peer shows the hub sitting in the middle in gold, which is the whole explanation.

Reach panel replaces header chips

"Double NAT" and "1 via hub" moved out of the 18px header chips into a panel that says what is wrong, what it costs in milliseconds, and the one action that fixes it.

1Baseline — the app as it is built today

Where I started, and what I am assuming

Read from the code

Everything below is rebuilt from src/index.css, tailwind.config.js and the screen files — the real tokens (base #0C0F14, gold #F5A623, cyan #39D9C8), the real radii (6/10/16), Geist, 44px header rows, 208px sidebar, and the sample peers from sim.ts. No invented values.

The problem I am designing against

The peer card carries eight facts in 248px — name, OS, layer, scope, latency, loss, address, port — plus five actions on a 32px row. Nothing has room, and the one thing that actually explains a bad connection (the path the traffic takes, and why) is compressed into a 9px badge.

What comes next

Turn 1 is the faithful baseline so we are arguing about the same thing. Turn 2 (coming next) takes the peer card and the relay/NAT story apart in three directions, then carries the winner across Files, Theatre, Games and the call — desktop and mobile, in the app's own language and in Modernist.
